# CaseKit Discovery

Prevent the team from solving an attractive version of the wrong problem.

## Choose depth

- `Sprint`: under 8 hours; ask the 3 highest-risk unanswered questions and produce one premise table.
- `Standard`: typical competition; complete the full interrogation and compare 3 opportunity frames.
- `Deep`: final round or high-stakes case; add interviews/observation, disconfirming evidence, and independent challenge.

Default to `Standard`. Use `Sprint` when the deadline demands it. Read `references/discovery-method.md` before running Standard or Deep mode. Read `references/interview-gate.md` when direct customer access exists.

## Interrogate one question at a time

Skip questions already answered with concrete evidence. Probe vague answers until they become observable:

1. What decision does the brief require, and what would success change?
2. When did the problem last happen to a specific person or organization?
3. What do they do today, and what does that workaround cost in money, time, risk, or missed outcome?
4. Who feels the pain, who uses the solution, who pays, and who can block adoption?
5. What is the narrowest intervention that changes the desired behavior or outcome?
6. What evidence would show the team is wrong?
7. Why is this team, channel, asset, timing, or ecosystem unusually able to win?

Do not ask for all answers in one batch when an interactive conversation is possible. After each answer, summarize the new premise, confidence, and implication.

## Challenge premises

Create 5–10 explicit premises with `PRM` IDs. Classify each as customer, problem, behavior, distribution, economics, feasibility, regulation, or right-to-win. For each premise record evidence, confidence, decision impact, falsification test, and owner.

Generate three frames:

- `Narrow`: smallest evidence-backed wedge.
- `Core`: strongest balanced competition recommendation.
- `10x`: ambitious outcome that remains causally credible.

Compare rubric fit, evidence, value, differentiation, feasibility, speed, economics, and risk. Choose one and record why the others were deferred.

## Gate

Return one verdict:

- `Proceed`: problem and stakeholder are sufficiently evidenced.
- `Proceed with validation`: direction is plausible but one or more critical premises remain unproven.
- `Reframe`: evidence supports a different problem, stakeholder, or mechanism.
- `Stop`: consequence is weak, status quo is adequate, or required assumptions are implausible.

Never force a positive verdict. Write unvalidated beliefs into the Assumption Ledger and validation actions into the Experiment backlog.
