# Five-Why numeric defense

## Method

Select the 3–5 inputs with highest sensitivity or judge scrutiny. For each input, ask:

1. Why is this number important to the result?
2. Why is this value or range appropriate here?
3. Why is the evidence or analogy transferable to this audience/channel/offer?
4. Why should the mechanism produce that behavior operationally?
5. Why is the downside bounded, and how will the team learn or respond?

Stop earlier when the chain reaches a contractual value, direct measurement, authoritative statistic, arithmetic identity, or capacity limit. Continue past five when a hidden assumption remains.

## Judge-answer pattern

```text
We use X as a base case, with a range of L–H. The anchor is [source/measurement], but that context differs in [dimension], so we adjusted [direction/magnitude]. X is one of the top sensitivity drivers: if it falls to L, the outcome becomes Y. We will validate it through [test] by [time], and our fallback is [action].
```

Do not defend a target as if it were evidence. Say: “That is our target; the model currently forecasts X, so the plan requires Y improvement in driver Z.”
