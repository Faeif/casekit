# Positioning method

Build in order:

1. Competitive alternatives: what the customer would do without this solution, including status quo.
2. Unique attributes: capabilities or assets alternatives lack today.
3. Value themes: translate each attribute into a measurable customer outcome.
4. Best-fit customer: identify who cares most because of context, constraints, trigger, or current spend.
5. Market category: choose the frame that gives buyers useful expectations and makes the value legible.
6. Trend overlay: add only when a current structural change materially increases urgency or advantage.

## Stress tests

- Relative truth: is the claimed advantage true against the customer's real alternative?
- Outcome test: does it explain why the customer should care?
- Proof test: can the team demonstrate it now?
- Memory test: can it be repeated accurately after one hearing?
- Misread test: what incorrect expectation could the category or tagline create?
- Category-cost test: does a new category require more education than the team can afford?

## Output

Produce the positioning statement, alternative map, category options with trade-offs, messaging hierarchy, words to use/avoid, proof points, objections, and test plan. Keep aspirational vision separate from current positioning.

## Roots and adaptation

The sequencing is informed by April Dunford's positioning approach and the public workflow in [startup-positioning](https://github.com/ferdinandobons/startup-skill/tree/main/startup-positioning). This document is an original competition-focused adaptation; see repository references and notices.
