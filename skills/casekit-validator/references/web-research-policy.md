# Web research and source-control policy

## Search process

1. Convert the decision into a claim list. Mark each claim `must prove`, `useful context`, or `optional`.
2. Name the ideal source before searching: regulator, official statistic, filing, technical documentation, original study, transaction data, interview, or independent analysis.
3. Search targeted domains and document types. Examples: `site:go.th filetype:pdf`, company `annual report`, regulator `consultation`, product `pricing`, repository `release notes`.
4. Open the original page. Never cite the result snippet or an AI summary.
5. Capture publisher, title, URL, published/updated date, accessed date, page/section, exact supporting passage or table, interpretation, and limits.
6. Triangulate material claims with an independent source or a different evidence method.
7. Run a disconfirming search: failure, complaint, limitation, churn, rejection, regulation, competing explanation, or contrary estimate.
8. Stop when additional research is unlikely to change the decision; log the residual uncertainty.

## Preferred source by claim

| Claim | Preferred evidence |
|---|---|
| Law/regulation | statute, regulator, official gazette, ministry |
| Population/economy | national statistics office, central bank, World Bank, OECD |
| Company performance | audited filing, investor report, official disclosure |
| Product capability/pricing | official documentation, pricing page, contract, release notes |
| Technical feasibility | official documentation, standards body, reproducible prototype/test |
| Market size | original dataset/report plus transparent bottom-up model |
| Customer problem | observed behavior, transaction/usage data, interviews with concrete events |
| Conversion benchmark | same funnel definition, channel, segment, geography, and period where possible |
| Sentiment | reviews/interviews/community as qualitative evidence only |
| Academic/health claim | peer-reviewed original research or systematic review; official health authority |

## Freshness

Verify current status for rules, prices, people, product features, software, market conditions, and schedules. Record a stricter refresh trigger when the claim can change quickly. Older evidence may remain valid for stable mechanisms, but explain why it transfers to the current context.

## Quality questions

- Is this the original source?
- Does it support the exact claim and denominator?
- Is the population/context transferable?
- Is the date fit for the decision?
- Is the method visible and credible?
- Does the publisher have an incentive to bias the result?
- What evidence would contradict this claim?

Use `quality`, `recency`, and `relevance` as separate judgments. A prestigious source can still be irrelevant to the target segment.
